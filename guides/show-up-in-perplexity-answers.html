How to show up in Perplexity answers

A practical guide for local businesses: how Perplexity picks the sources it cites, and the seven things that help your business get named. No paid placement, no tricks.

How Perplexity picks sources

Perplexity is an answer engine, not a list of links. When someone asks for the best dentist or plumber nearby, it searches the web, reads a handful of pages, and writes an answer with inline citations to the pages it used. The pages it trusts most are rarely the business’s own website. They are review sites, directories, local news, “best of” lists, Reddit threads, and YouTube videos. Your own site matters, but it is one voice among many. That is the whole game: Perplexity answers are a reputation contest fought on other people’s websites, and the winner is usually the business with the most consistent, recent, third-party proof.

1. Answer the question on the page

Perplexity looks for pages that directly answer what was asked. If your page opens with a clear, direct answer — the service, the price, the area you serve — it is easier to cite than a page that buries the answer under a slogan. Put the answer in the first paragraph, in plain words, and keep the page short. A page titled “How much does a root canal cost in Austin?” is more citable than a page titled “Endodontic Services”.

2. Keep your pages fresh

Perplexity favours recent pages. A service page updated this year is more likely to be cited than the same page untouched since 2022, because the answer engine treats recency as a sign the information is still true. Update your hours, prices and service area when they change, and refresh your main pages at least once a year. A date on the page helps, but only if the content actually changed.

3. Get cited where Perplexity already looks

Every category has its own shortlist of sources. For dentists it is Google reviews, Yelp, Healthgrades and Zocdoc. For lawyers it is Avvo, Justia and FindLaw. For plumbers it is Google, Yelp, Angi and HomeAdvisor. For restaurants it is Google, Yelp, TripAdvisor and OpenTable. For real estate agents it is Zillow and Realtor.com. Being absent from the top two or three sources for your trade is the single most common reason a good business never gets cited. Claim every profile, fill it in completely, add real photos, and keep the details identical.

4. Reviews are the currency

Reviews do two jobs at once. They tell the answer engine that real customers exist and liked you, and they give it fresh text to quote. Recent, genuine reviews tend to count for more than a pile of old ones, even at the same rating, because recency appears to be a signal the search trusts. Ask every happy customer, in person, right after the job is done. Make it easy with a link or a QR code at the counter. Reply to reviews, good and bad, in your own words. And never buy reviews or write your own: fake reviews are easy to spot, and getting caught costs more than having few.

5. Structure pages so they can be quoted

Perplexity quotes sentences, not whole pages. Short paragraphs, clear headings, lists and tables are easier to lift into an answer than long blocks of prose. Use headings that match the questions customers ask, put one fact per line in lists, and add FAQ schema to your FAQ page so the questions and answers are machine-readable. A page that a person can skim in ten seconds is usually a page an answer engine can quote.

6. Earn mentions you cannot buy

The strongest signal is someone else writing about you without being paid to. A local news story, a “best of” list, a neighborhood blog, a Reddit thread where a real customer recommends you by name. You cannot buy these, but you can earn them: do work worth mentioning, invite a local writer in, answer a reporter’s question, sponsor one neighborhood event and let the organizer say so. A genuine mention on a trusted local site often helps more than many thin directory listings, because it is the kind of page Perplexity loves to cite.

7. Check, fix one thing, check again

Perplexity answers change from run to run, so a single check is a snapshot, not a verdict. The useful habit is a loop: run a check, find the one source you are missing from or the one fix that matters most, fix it, and check again in a week or two. Do not try to fix everything at once. FAQ

Does Perplexity recommend local businesses?

Yes. When asked for the best business in a city, Perplexity searches the web and names specific businesses with citations. Which ones depends on reviews, directories and local lists. Prisqo measures this with one AI model with live web search as a stand-in; it does not query Perplexity directly.

How is Perplexity different from ChatGPT?

Perplexity is built as an answer engine: it shows its sources inline for nearly every claim, and it leans more on recent pages and community sources. ChatGPT search also cites sources, but its answers are shaped by its own index and search partners. The fixes that help are mostly the same: consistent listings, recent reviews, and pages that answer real questions.
